# Music-learning and practice software: interaction patterns, evidence, and transfer to an oud / pentatonic / Somali-rhythm browser studio (as of 2025–2026)

Scope note. Research run 2026-11-07. Product facts are from vendor help centres, app-store listings and 2019–2026 press/reviews; research facts are from abstracts and (where fetchable) full texts. Anything older than 2019 is dated inline. "Vendor claim" marks statements that come only from the maker. Where a number could not be sourced it is in the Gaps list, not in the text. The session's web-search budget ran out after the third wave, so a handful of planned confirmations (listed under Gaps) were not made.

## Key question 1 — Pattern inventory: what the mainstream apps actually do (Yousician, Simply Piano, Flowkey, Melodics, Soundslice, Moises, Anytune, Transcribe!, Chrome Music Lab, Ableton Learning Music/Synths, ToneGym/Functional Ear Trainer, Duolingo Music, GarageBand)

### Takeaway
Across the category there are only about eight recurring mechanics: (1) scrolling or page notation with a playhead, (2) real-time note detection colouring each target green/red with a timing label, (3) a "wait" or step mode that halts the playhead until the right note sounds, (4) tempo reduction with pitch preserved (floors range from 75 % in piano apps to 5 % in transcription tools), with "auto-tempo" that raises speed only after clean passes, (5) section looping snapped to notes/bars, (6) transposition of notation and audio together, (7) streak/multiplier scoring with stars, and (8) a progress graph per song. None of the vendors publish their hit-window or pitch-tolerance numbers; the only published timing windows in this space come from open rhythm games (e.g. osu!taiko: ±50 ms "GREAT" at the easiest setting down to ±20 ms at the hardest).

### Cited Findings

**Yousician (guitar/piano/bass/uke/voice; microphone or MIDI)**
- Third-party reviews describe the core loop: the app listens via the microphone, correctly timed notes/chords light green, and each note is labelled perfect / great / good / early / late; consecutive correct notes build a multiplier up to 5× and points roll up into stars (reviewer descriptions, not vendor spec) — [Beginner Guitar HQ review](https://beginnerguitarhq.com/yousician-guitar-review); [American Songwriter review](https://americansongwriter.com/yousician-review/); [StriveCloud case study](https://strivecloud.io/?p=325033).
- Official practice tools: "When you select Auto-adjust speed from Options, Yousician automatically adjusts the song speed — slowing down or speeding up based on how you play. You can also set the tempo manually using the tempo slider." — [Yousician support: best way to practice piano](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005259945-The-best-way-to-practice-piano).
- Looping: "Move the sliders to loop difficult sections. This way, you can really focus on a specific trouble spot." — [Yousician support: best way to practice singing](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000555665-The-best-way-to-practice-singing).
- Update 4.14 (2020) replaced the slider on the main practice screen with ±5 % tempo buttons and moved Auto tempo to the pause menu: "The song tempo can also now be changed from the main song practice screen in increments of 5%." — [Yousician blog, update 4.14](https://yousician.com/blog/whats-new-in-yousician-update-4-14).
- Practice vs Play modes are documented per instrument (the guitar article returned HTTP 403 when fetched; piano/bass/singing variants exist) — [Practice vs Play mode (piano)](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/204796991-Practice-and-Play-modes-in-piano); [Practice vs Play mode (singing)](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000553289-Practice-and-Play-modes-in-singing).
- Known weakness: chord recognition reported as inconsistent on some guitar setups by learners — [JustinGuitar community thread](https://community.justinguitar.com/t/my-concern-with-the-way-the-app-works/75193).
- Repertoire is licensed and can disappear: "due to licensing agreements, content availability is subject to change; sometimes, songs can be removed" — [Yousician support: why can't I find a popular song](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000758937-Why-can-t-I-find-a-popular-song-in-Yousician); licensed popular songs arrived in a 2018 update — [Wikipedia: Yousician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousician).

**Simply Piano (JoyTunes/Simply)**
- Recognition hygiene the vendor asks for: wear headphones so the mic does not hear the backing track; tune to A = 440 Hz; prefer a MIDI cable for keyboards ("we highly recommend using a MIDI connection to improve the accuracy of your note recognition") — [Simply help: acoustic piano recognition](https://piano-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/6210583-ways-to-improve-note-recognition-in-simply-piano-for-ios-acoustic-piano); [Simply help: keyboard recognition](https://piano-help.hellosimply.com/en/articles/6210608-ways-to-improve-note-recognition-in-simply-piano-for-ios-keyboard-electronic-piano).
- The Simply Piano 3.0 announcement describes an automatic slow-down rather than a user-toggled wait mode: making a mistake twice in a song switches the app into a slower practice state, signalled by a white metronome icon top-right, "so you can learn the notes at a slower pace" (from search excerpt of the post; the page itself timed out on fetch) — [Simply Piano 3.0 blog](https://old.hellosimply.com/blog/?p=3771).
- I found no Simply help article documenting a manual "wait mode"; a user review mentions "Wait mode sometime skipped", which implies the mode exists but is undocumented — [Simply help centre search results](https://piano-help.hellosimply.com:443/en/collections/3479769-common-questions).
- 2026: Simply added Oxford University Press piano-method sheet music (educational-publisher deal, not a pop licence) — [OUP announcement](https://corp.oup.com/?p=6927).

**Flowkey**
- Three separate practice tools: Wait Mode ("listens to your playing and waits for you to hit the right notes"), Slow Motion ("Play at reduced speed to get comfortable with a piece") and Loop ("Replay a specific section until you perfect it") — [Yamaha flowkey page](https://au.yamaha.com/en/products/contents/pianos/flowkey/).
- Only two slow speeds: "Choose to play the right hand alone … this will prompt … Wait mode, plus a choice of two tempos – 50% and 75%." — [Pianist Magazine](https://pianistmagazine.com/blogs/learn-all-the-songs-that-you-love-with-flowkey); "'Wait Mode' will only play through a selected section, the self-paced feature offers '50%' and '75%' speed for the whole piece." — [Yamaha 2023 news](https://au.yamaha.com/en/musical-instruments/pianos/news/2023/flowkey-extension-6.html).
- Repertoire split: the "Classic" plan covers "more than 1,000 classical and public-domain pieces … plus courses covering music theory, chords, rhythms, technique, and reading sheet music"; Premium adds the licensed Pop/Rock/Film catalogue — [flowkey Classic plan listing](https://deals.androidauthority.com/sales/flowkey-classic-plan-3-year-subscription); 5 million users claimed — [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/deals/the-flowkey-piano-app-has-5-million-users-and-a-67-99-price-tag/).

**Melodics (pads, keys, e-drums; MIDI input)**
- Scoring rules (vendor): "Melodics scores your lessons based on timing precision and note streaks." Each note is measured for closeness to the beat — "A note that lands tightly on time scores higher than one that was only within the hit window." A missed note resets the streak, "so consistency matters as much as accuracy." Stars: one, two, three or Platinum; Platinum "requires a perfect, all-green performance" but is "a checkpoint" not a ceiling. Scores are uncapped ("scores can go beyond 100"); a dense lesson can score 10,000+, a short one ~1,000, so the vendor tells users to benchmark against themselves per lesson — [Melodics: Lesson scoring](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/15092017-lesson-scoring).
- Practice Mode (vendor): BPM slider "right down to a stop"; **Auto BPM raises tempo by 10 BPM at a time "if you're nailing it"** until the track's natural tempo; Loop with keyboard shortcuts `;` `'` for start and `[` `]` for end; **Step-by-step (Wait Mode): "When a note reaches the playhead, the performance pauses until you play the correct note."** No accuracy percentage or pass/fail criterion is defined — [Melodics: Practice mode](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/6777027-practice-mode).
- Colour code in press material: green = correct timing, red = missed — [Melodics press clip](https://melodics.rockpaperscissors.biz/artist/19108/press_clips/28857/white).
- No millisecond hit window is published anywhere I could find — [Melodics support search](https://www.melodics.com/support-articles/support-scoring).

**Soundslice (browser; notation + audio/video sync)**
- Feature set: synced notation/tab with audio or YouTube/Vimeo/uploaded video; click a note to jump the video; drag across notation to make a loop that "snaps to the nearest note, rest, or barline"; slowdown without pitch change via ± buttons or typed value; transposition applies to notation, tab (with estimated fingerings) and audio together — [Soundslice features](https://www.soundslice.com/features/); [Soundslice player help index](https://soundslice.com/help/en/player) (lists separate articles: Looping, Playback speed, Speed training, Metronome and count-in, Transposing, Adjusting videos, Adjusting latency).
- Enhanced slowdown + perfect looping (2020s blog): applies only to MP3-synced slices, not video; audio keeps playing during speed changes "and the speed smoothly transitions"; MP3 loops are "perfectly timed" with "no more split-second gaps between loop iterations"; falls back to the legacy algorithm on old browsers/iOS where it may cause "choppy audio" — [Soundslice blog 199](https://www.soundslice.com/blog/199/introducing-enhanced-slowdown-and-perfect-looping/).
- A partner site notes video looping has "a slight delay between loops, depending on video quality and loop length", while audio-only loops are precise — [JazzEdge Academy](https://jazzedge.academy/?p=3812). A search excerpt of Soundslice help says some browsers reach 25 % speed (not re-verified on the page itself) — [Soundslice features](https://www.soundslice.com/features/).

**Moises (AI practice app)**
- App Store listing (vendor): stem separation (vocals/drums/bass free; guitar, other instruments and drum parts on paid tiers), chord detection synced to bars and beats, key detection with transposition to all 12 keys, pitch shifter, "1-click slow down or speed up for tricky sections", smart metronome, count-in, loop via trim, BPM detection — [Moises on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/1515796612).

**Anytune (iOS/macOS slow-downer)**
- Tempo without pitch change "down to 0.05x"; pitch shift up to 24 semitones; unlimited loops and marks; **"Step-it-Up Loop Interval Trainer" repeats a loop while gradually changing speed**; count-in; ReFrame isolation and FineTouch EQ in paid tiers; cannot process DRM (Apple Music) tracks — [Anytune on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id415365180); [Anytune Pro+ review](https://spencerbruce.com/blog1/anytune-pro-review?format=amp).

**Transcribe! (Seventh String; Win/Mac/Linux)**
- Speed "from one twentieth to double speed", pitch unchanged, processed on the fly; half-speed button; markers for sections/measures/beats with labels; "store and recall any number of named loops"; pitch shift in cents and up to three octaves; EQ; spectrum display over a piano keyboard with note and chord guesses (e.g. "E7#9") shown as piano-roll over time; video display; foot-pedal control; 30-day free evaluation. "It doesn't transcribe automatically" — [Transcribe! overview](https://www.seventhstring.com/xscribe/overview.html).

**Chrome Music Lab (Google; browser, free)**
- Song Maker "lets you make and share your own songs"; teachers use the Lab to connect music with science, maths, art and dance — [Chrome Music Lab experiments](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Experiments).
- Song Maker's settings panel offers a **pentatonic scale option**; school lesson plans have pupils switch to pentatonic and compose on C D E G A — [TeachSimple lesson](https://teachsimple.com/product/chrome-music-lab-1-1_-pentatonic-scale-my-name); [Dobcroft Junior School plan](https://dobcroft-jun.sheffield.sch.uk/serve_file/956805).
- Design consequence noted by a reviewer: Song Maker "restricts which notes are available based on the scale chosen, so most results sound consistent regardless of skill" — [Alphr review](https://www.alphr.com/google/1008703/google-song-maker-chrome-musicians/). Drum sounds: Electronic, Blocks, Kit, Conga — [Gigazine](https://wbgsv0a.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20180302-chrome-music-lab-song-maker).
- The Lab also lists Rhythm, Melody Maker, Harmonics, Oscillators, Shared Piano, Spectrogram etc. (names only; the experiments page's descriptions did not render for the fetcher) — [Skoove blog list](https://www.skoove.com/blog/?p=8762).

**Ableton Learning Music (2017) and Learning Synths (2019)**
- Free browser courses; Learning Synths moves "step by step through synth basics, ending in a 'playground'"; lessons use "simple monochrome geometric shapes that users manipulate to change sound"; a browser two-oscillator monosynth playable from MIDI in Chrome; export to a Live Set — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2019/06/25/ableton-learning-synths-synthesizer-lessons/); [Ableton blog](https://www.ableton.com/es/blog/learn-synthesis-in-your-browser/); [CDM on Ableton Learn](https://cdm.link/ableton-learn/).

**ToneGym and Functional Ear Trainer (ear training)**
- ToneGym: "gamified ear training platform … Train intervals, chords, scales, rhythm, melody, and sight-reading through musical games and daily workouts"; pricing reported as US$13.95–14.95/month or $74.95–79/year (sources differ) — [ToneGym about](https://tonegym.co/site/about); [MusicRadar](https://musicradar.com/news/tonegym-is-an-online-ear-training-platform-that-could-help-you-to-master-music-theory-and-write-better-songs); [Sonicstate 2023](https://sonicstate.com/news/2023/03/30/improve-your-musical-skills).
- Functional Ear Trainer implements Alain Benbassat's method: a cadence establishes the key, then the learner names a scale degree — "it teaches you to distinguish between tones in the context of a particular musical key" — [Functional Ear Trainer (App Store)](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1088761926); [SourceForge listing](https://sourceforge.net/app/functional-ear-trainer/android/). A competitor's blog (marketing, treat as such) calls the cadence-then-degree drill "the canonical functional-ear exercise" — [Fifths blog](https://fifths-marketing.pages.dev/blog/best-ear-training-apps/).
- Neither vendor documents spaced repetition; ToneGym's "daily workouts" is the closest construct — [ToneGym education page](https://www.tonegym.co/site/education).

**Duolingo Music (2023–)**
- Vendor design post: exercises are game-like (no long videos); note-finding on an on-screen keyboard from a staff; drag the right note/rest onto the staff; high-vs-low listening; content "starting with individual notes and moving toward full-length songs", organised in units on a learning path (e.g. "Unit 1: Learn the notes C, D, and E"); "all of our lessons are based on a piano keyboard"; Music XP counts toward Daily Quests, achievements and the streak — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/p/fc0b9772-e1f6-4223-9a9f-ebf76b94b047/).
- A learner who used it four months beside real lessons: "Within a month or two I was able to skip an entire beginner's course my tutor and I were going through" (anecdote) — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/websites-apps/ive-been-using-duolingo-music-for-4-months-alongside-actual-piano-lessons-heres-what-i-found); a critic notes it lacks the explanatory "theory notes" other Duolingo courses have — [Rocknerd review](https://rocknerd.co.uk/2024/11/12/duolingo-music-review/); a 2025 update reportedly added an instruments tab for practising on a real piano — [Duolingo Guides](https://duolingoguides.com/how-to-get-duolingo-music/).

**GarageBand "Learn to Play" / "How Did I Play" (macOS)**
- Apple: each completed performance gets a percentage; History shows each performance as a point on a graph; High Scores lists top performances "each showing error areas"; Reset Lesson deletes score history and recordings — [GarageBand User Guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/garageband/gbnd50355d19/mac).
- Colour code (search excerpt of the sibling guide page): correct = green, wrong notes = red, timing errors = light green in a row above the control bar; "My Results" mutes your take and hides errors — [GarageBand guide page](https://support.apple.com/guide/garageband/gbndb71d9eec/mac); [Dummies walkthrough](https://www.dummies.com/article/technology/software/music-recording-software/garageband-music-recording-software/learn-to-play-an-instrument-in-garageband-151494/).

**Published timing windows (from open rhythm games, the only concrete numbers available)**
- osu!taiko judgement windows are half-widths in ms as a function of Overall Difficulty (OD 0–10): GREAT = 50 − 3·OD (50 → 20 ms); OK = 120 − 8·OD for OD ≤ 5, 110 − 6·OD for OD ≥ 5 (120 → 50 ms); MISS = 135 − 8·OD / 120 − 5·OD (135 → 70 ms) — [osu!taiko judgement](https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Gameplay/Judgement/osu!taiko).
- Clone Hero: "At any point when the gem is inside of the hit window, pressing the correct fret will count as 'hit'"; the window can be displayed and widened via lower difficulty or "Drunk Mode"; no ms value published — [Clone Hero manual](https://wiki.clonehero.net/books/clone-hero-manual/page/how-to-play/revisions/900).

### Inferences
- The *minimal viable* Learn/Play loop that every successful app shares is: notation-with-playhead + per-note green/red + wait mode + 50/75 % slow-down + loop + per-song progress. A browser studio that already has a DAW and a YIN pitch tracker can implement all six without new signal processing.
- Tempo floors tell you the audience: 50–75 % (Flowkey) for sheet-music learners, 25 % (Soundslice) for players, 5 % (Transcribe!, Anytune) for by-ear transcription. An oral-tradition "learn by ear" mode should go at least to 25 % and expose a one-tap half-speed button like Transcribe!.
- Melodics' two published rules (score ∝ closeness to beat inside a hit window; a miss resets the streak) plus Auto BPM +10 BPM-on-clean-pass are a complete, copyable spec for a rhythm Play mode. For hit windows, osu!taiko's OD 0–5 range (±50–35 ms "great", ±120–80 ms "ok") is a defensible default ladder for beginners; tighten with level.
- Chrome Music Lab's "restrict the grid to the scale" is the simplest pentatonic-first design: hide the two non-scale degrees, make it impossible to be wrong at level 1, and reveal them later.

### Gaps
- No vendor (Yousician, Simply, Flowkey, Melodics, GarageBand) publishes its pitch tolerance (cents) or timing window (ms); the perfect/great/good/early/late labels for Yousician come from reviewers, not documentation.
- The Yousician Practice-vs-Play guitar article and Simply Piano 3.0 post could not be fetched (403/504); their content is from search excerpts.
- Chrome Music Lab's Rhythm and Spectrogram experiments: the official page did not render descriptions; details are from memory-free secondary lists only, so no specifics are recorded here.
- Soundslice's exact speed floor/step and transposition range sit in sub-articles that were not fetched.

## Key question 2 — Non-Western and oral-tradition pedagogy software (maqam/oud, Hindustani/Carnatic, Turkish makam, Chinese instruments): how tonic, cents and rhythmic cycles are presented, and whether any of it has been evaluated

### Takeaway
The non-Western tools converge on three presentation choices the Western apps lack: an explicit, user-set tonic (shruti/sa, maqam tonic) against which everything is measured; per-note deviation in cents rather than right/wrong note names; and rhythm taught as named cycles with stroke syllables (dum/tak, theka bols, solkattu) rather than as time signatures. Research prototypes exist (CompMusic/Dunya for raga and makam, Uyar & Bozkurt's usul trainer, an erhu pitch-and-rhythm tutor) but I found no controlled learning-outcome evaluation for any of them; the commercial apps' accuracy claims are unverified vendor claims.

### Cited Findings

**Arabic maqam / oud**
- Maqam World (Johnny Farraj; online since ~2001, rebuilt 2016, companion OUP book *Inside Arabic Music* 2019) presents theory as **ajnas** (3–5-note scale fragments), each with a **tonic** ("the note of main melodic emphasis, usually the first note"), a leading tone and a **ghammaz** ("the next most important note of emphasis and the most common starting point for a new jins"); a maqam is "really a pathway among many ajnas"; the basic scale (tonic → ghammaz) is drawn in large notes, "baggage" notes smaller; notes are clickable to sound — [Maqam World home](https://www.maqamworld.com/en/); [Maqam World: jins](https://www.maqamworld.com/en/jins.php).
- Rhythm (**iqa'at**) is taught as a "prototypal measure" built from two sounds, *dum* ("bassy and sustained") and *tak* ("dry and sharp"); the notated iqa' "is a skeleton" that percussionists ornament. Cycles are indexed alphabetically and by beats per measure, and grouped by use: 2/4, 3/4, 4/4, 6/4, 8/4 "mostly used in folk music, mid-20th-century tarab, and contemporary popular music", long cycles (7/8 … 32/4) in Muwashahat. Examples: 2/4 Ayyub, Malfuf, Fallahi, Wahda Saghira; 3/4 Sama'i Darij; **6/8 Yuruk Semai**; 4/4 Maqsum, Baladi, Sa'idi, Wahda; 10/8 Sama'i Thaqil, Jurjina — [Maqam World: iqa'](https://www.maqamworld.com/en/iqaa.php).
- Oud/maqam apps (all vendor claims from listings): *Maqam Oud* shows maqam notes with quarter-tone positions marked "which helps with finger placement on the neck", works offline, inserts breaks between training rounds — [Maqam Oud listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/maqam-oud/6795169945); *Bonjour Maqami* is iPad maqam ear training "focused on microtonal scales" — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6760976540); *Oriental Music Academy* added "an ear-training studio with intonation drills, same-or-different listening, and drone practice" — [AppsHunter listing](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/oriental-music-academy/id491995897); *Makam Studio* (Turkish) uses "100% real studio recordings and color-coded tablature" for rhythm — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6759348598).
- Pedagogical sequencing from practitioners: start with maqamat that avoid quarter tones (Nahawand, Hijaz) before Bayati and Rast — [Mike's Oud Forums](https://www.mikeouds.com/messageboard/viewthread.php?tid=12356); Oud for Guitarists' ear-training programme is "Hear a Maqam – Sing a maqam – Know the maqam", i.e. sing-back before play-back — [Oud for Guitarists ear training](https://www.oudforguitarists.com/ear-training-program/).

**Indian art music (Riyaz and tala apps)**
- Riyaz (MusicMuni Labs, Bengaluru) — vendor: "Our real-time pitch detector guides you to the right note, whether you're singing Carnatic, Hindustani, or Pop"; during sing-along "the original singer's pitches" are drawn alongside the user's pitch in real time, showing higher/lower/same; a **Smart Tanpura** mode shows the free-sung contour with no reference; Hindustani/Carnatic learners "choose a raag and set a shruti (base note)"; the app "extracts pitch information … compares it to the original singer's pitches, and scores the user" (no scale or threshold given) — [Riyaz](https://riyazapp.com); [Riyaz blog: a mirror](https://riyazapp.com/blog/a-mirror-youve-never-seen-before/); the vendor also claims feedback on "pitch accuracy, timing, and pronunciation" — [Riyaz blog: lockdown](https://riyazapp.com/blog/how-riyaz-helped-music-students-during-lockdown/); press describes it as comparing the student to a guru's rendition — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/weekender/music-learning-app-riyaz-hits-right-note-self-learners-technology).
- A deliberately non-judgemental alternative: *Daily Riyaz – Pitch Visualizer* advertises "no scores, no judgments, and no pressure" — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.rahuls.riyaz).
- Tala tools present rhythm as cycles with strokes and tempo bands: *Laya Tarang* plays "tabla teka at a fixed laya with a metronome" for layakari practice — [Laya Tarang](https://www.bluestacks.com/campaign/com.siddhisadhana.layatarang/en); *iTabla Sangat* offers Teen Taal, Ek Taal etc. with theka variations and laya from vilambit to ati-vilambit — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6496281720); *Tāl Chakra* maps "tempo against density across five laya zones" — [listing](https://mwm.ai/pt/apps/tl-chakra/6762078652); Carnatic: *TaalaVerse* (solkattu, tempo control, laya exercises) — [AppsHunter](https://appshunter.io/ios/app/taalaverse-carnatic-rhythm/id6774167269); *Shruti Laya* plays tanpura + mridangam + manjira on the seven suladi sapta talas — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1497345955); *Tala Keeper* is a visual metronome with Carnatic presets — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id614784414); a free self-study trainer *Tala Anubhava* (Sundaresan & Pesch, 2024) teaches by reciting syllables with a teacher's audio — [carnaticstudent.org PDF](https://www.carnaticstudent.org/wp-content/uploads/TALA_ANUBHAVA_EN.pdf); [practice four talas](https://carnaticstudent.org/practice-four-talas).

**CompMusic / Dunya (UPF, 2011–2017) and Turkish makam**
- Dunya is "a collection of music corpora and related software tools" for exploring recordings, "aimed at music connoisseurs" — [Dunya](https://dunya.compmusic.upf.edu); the 2014 mid-term report says "we have not yet started any formal evaluation of research results using Dunya" — [CompMusic mid-term](https://compmusic.upf.edu/te/node/204); the final report frames Dunya as the vehicle "to evaluate our research results through user scenarios" — [CORDIS final report](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/267583/reporting/es).
- UPF's education framing: "design learning methodologies with which people can improve their listening abilities", covering "tāla cycles, melodic movement among the svaras of particular rāgas, or the melodic contour and structural function of characteristic phrases" — [CompMusic seminar](https://compmusic.upf.edu/hi/node/211); a 2018 UPF deck lists the benefit as bridging "the gap between theory and sound by visualization", interactivity and self-pacing — [Caro, Musical Bridges 2018](https://www.upf.edu:443/documents/178510705/220477489/20180922-Caro-MusicalBridges-SASMR2018.pdf/e06fb730-852c-5817-b07e-d095a0e541bd).
- Uyar & Bozkurt (FMA 2015): an interactive **usul** trainer for home practice — the student records a performance, the system "automatically detects onsets in the recordings and compares their locations" to the usul template with a method the authors define; reported only as "a demonstration of the software and preliminary test results on basic rhythmic patterns"; motivation: makam education "is carried out by face-to-face sessions with the masters" — [Zenodo PDF](https://zenodo.org/records/1211661/files/FMA2015_BurakUyar_FullPaper.pdf); [MTG record](https://mtg.upf.edu/node/3874); the companion thesis aims to define "criteria for measuring rhythm similarity, which is generally evaluated perceptually" — [YÖK thesis record](https://acikbilim.yok.gov.tr/handle/20.500.12812/60036?show=full).

**Chinese instruments**
- Research prototype: "Development and Usability Evaluation of an Intelligent Personalized Erhu Pitch and Rhythm Learning System" (ICCE 2023) generates sheet music in MML, lets learners play along and tracks history; identifies pitch and rhythm as beginners' main errors; "Learners with more erhu playing experience showed higher acceptance of the system" (usability, not learning gain) — [APSCE ICCE](https://library.apsce.net/index.php/ICCE/article/view/1206).
- Apps (vendor): *Soloist Erhu* grades "each note in cents" with "fretless pitch feedback, per-note cents results, and phrase review" — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6776352481); *Erhu Vista* uses real-time pitch detection and fingerboard charts — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6739306930); *iGuzheng* "snaps the pitch slider to the correct grid" — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/iguzheng/id438675825?ls=1).

**Somali material relevant to the design**
- Somali songs are pentatonic: "they only use five pitches per octave in contrast to a heptatonic (seven note) scale such as the major scale" — [Wikipedia: Music of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Somalia); the oud is the *kaban*, introduced to Somali song by Abdullahi Qarshe; qaraami is the mid-20th-century urban song style "accompanied primarily by the kaban (oud)" — [Wikipedia: Qaraami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaraami).
- Dhaanto is "a fast, jubilant Somali folk song-and-dance tradition characterized by call-and-response vocals, driving handclaps, and synchronized line formations" — [Melodigging: dhaanto](https://www.melodigging.com/genre/dhaanto); a dance tutorial sets it "to a 4/4 time signature" with claps on beats 1 and 3 — [Medium: Dhaanto dance steps](https://medium.com/@thebluestars/dhaanto-dance-steps-d17b1ab0fa4f). A production guide found in search describes qaraami as "slow-to-mid tempi in 4/4 or a lilting 6/8" (secondary, unattributed) — see [Music of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Somalia) for the primary context.
- Buraanbur is a women's poetic genre "of a lighter measure (miisaan)" than gabay — [Cavacopedia: buraanbur](https://cavac.at/cavacopedia/buraanbur); Somali poetic metre is quantitative (vowel-length based) — [Orwin, metre in the geeraar (2018)](https://unora.unior.it//retrieve/handle/11574/199777/90124/Orwin%20-%202018%20-%20Metre%20and%20'extrametricality'%20in%20the%20geeraar.pdf); the melody–metre relation is the subject of "On the musical patterning of sculpted words" (African Music journal; full text not retrieved) — [African Music article](https://journal.ru.ac.za/index.php/africanmusic/article/view/1830) and of Hayward & Lewis, "Music, metre and Somali poetry" — [Taylor & Francis chapter](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/mono/10.4324/9780203985397-13/music-metre-somali-poetry-hayward-lewis).

### Inferences
- The right model for a Somali oud Learn mode is Riyaz + Maqam World, not Yousician: set the tonic first (the singer's/oud's *sa*), draw every pitch as cents-from-tonic on a pentatonic lattice, and treat the fitted scale (your Pitch tab already fits a pentatonic grid) as the reference rather than 12-TET note names. Pentatonic qaraami avoids maqam quarter tones, so the tolerance problem is simpler than for Bayati/Rast — the oud forum's "start without quarter tones" ordering is already satisfied.
- Teach rhythm the maqam-world/tala way: named cycles with two stroke colours (dum/tak ≈ low/high clap or oud bass/treble strokes), a notated skeleton, and a "fill/ornament" layer that is optional — not a 6/8 time-signature lesson. The 6/8 vs 4/4 question for dhaanto is unresolved in sources (see Gaps); the UI should allow a cycle to be displayed either as 2×3 or 4 pulses and let the musician-annotator pick per song.
- Sing-back before play-back (Oud for Guitarists, Riyaz) is the cheapest call-and-response step: the microphone pitch path is the same whether the learner sings or plays the kaban.
- No non-Western tool has published learning outcomes; a Somali tool that logs per-note cents and per-stroke ms over weeks would be the first to have measured data in this niche — a legitimate research claim.

### Gaps
- Uyar & Bozkurt's actual comparison method (alignment, tolerance, similarity measure) is in the PDF body, which could not be parsed locally (no PDF text tool available); only the abstract-level description is recorded.
- Maqam World does not state cents for microtones on the index pages fetched; the per-jins pages may.
- No source gives a time signature or beat cycle for buraanbur as music; the "lilting 6/8" qaraami claim is a single unattributed secondary line and conflicts with the 4/4 dance tutorial for dhaanto. A Somali musician or the Hayward & Lewis / African Music texts are needed.
- No evaluation of Riyaz's scoring accuracy or learning effect was found; MusicMuni's research papers were not reached before the search budget ran out.

## Key question 3 — What the research literature says: real-time visual pitch feedback, slowed playback and looping, pitch contours, spaced repetition, gamification, and the risks

### Takeaway
Real-time visual pitch feedback reliably improves accuracy *while it is on*, and the best recent controlled study (Blanco, Tassani & Ramirez 2021, n = 47 beginners, violin and voice) found that feedback **with explicit right/wrong information and a visible target** also produced retained gains at transfer, whereas matched-timbre auditory feedback helped only during acquisition; older and other studies (Welch 1989; Wilson 2007/2008; Zaki-Azat 2016; Paney & Tharp 2019; White 2025) range from positive to null, and the dependence worry is real but not consistently demonstrated. Interleaving/spacing evidence in music is small and mixed (helps retention, hurts in-session performance, learners dislike it); gamification evidence is engagement-level, not outcome-level; and no controlled study of slowed playback or looping for by-ear learning was found.

### Cited Findings

**Visual pitch feedback — positive and mixed results**
- Blanco, Tassani & Ramirez (2021, *Frontiers in Psychology*): 47 beginners randomised to Feedback Group (FG, n = 15: real-time visual pitch + knowledge of results; target as a yellow bar, played pitch as a green line), Equal-Timbre auditory group (ETG, n = 17), Control (n = 15), plus 15 expert violinists. Task: match five targets (D#4–G4) on the violin D string or by singing. **Post-aid absolute error: FG 14.73 cents (violin) / 20.45 cents (voice) vs ETG 169.24 / 145.36 cents.** Transfer-minus-baseline improvement: FG 81.17 cents (violin), 105.31 (voice); ETG 24 / −11. Correct notes (<50 cents) improved at transfer by 1.38 (violin) and 0.92 (voice) of 5 for FG vs −0.38 / 0.16 for ETG; control "did not match the notes even with unlimited hearing of the synthesized target". Baseline beginner errors ~150 cents (violin) and ~271 cents (voice). The ETG reached ~4.28/5 correct with voice while aided but did not retain it. Authors: objective pitch-distance information plus exploring the pitch space "strengthened participants' schemas and aided retention" — [Blanco et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693/full).
- Wilson (2007, Univ. of Sydney MA): "the two groups who had used real-time visual feedback demonstrated marked improvement on their initial pitch accuracy"; the two display styles did not differ; "cognitive load related to the decoding of visual information was a factor at intervention" — [Wilson thesis record](https://ses.library.usyd.edu.au/handle/2123/1526). Published as Wilson, Lee, Callaghan & Thorpe, "Learning to sing in tune: Does real-time visual feedback help?", *J. Interdisciplinary Music Studies* 2 (2008) — cited in [arXiv survey 2601.12153](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.12153).
- Welch, Howard & Rush (1989, *Psychology of Music*; dated): 32 children in interactive, non-interactive and control groups using the SINGAD system, reassessed after one school term; pilot results "support its use for the development of conscious pitch control in primary school children" — [Welch et al. 1989](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0305735689172005); [Howard & Welch, Eurospeech 1989](https://www.isca-archive.org/eurospeech_1989/howard89b_eurospeech.html); the IOA conference paper PDF was 404 at fetch time — [IOA SINGAD paper](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/GF%20WELCH%20C%20RUSH%20D%20HOWARD%20THE%20SINGAD%20SINGING%20ASSESSMENT%20AND%20DEVELOPMENT%20SYSTEM%20FIRST%20APPLICATIONS%20IN%20THE%20CLASSROOM.pdf).
- Welch's schema account: singing skill "require[s] external right/wrong feedback [knowledge of results (KR)] at the beginning of the learning process", and visual feedback *without* KR did not improve pitch matching — as summarised in [Blanco et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693/full) and a [2025 Frontiers paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1521407/full).

**Null results and the dependence question**
- Zaki-Azat (2016, Wilfrid Laurier thesis): "there was no effect of real-time visual feedback training or length of training on measures of vocal control" — [WLU thesis](https://scholars.wlu.ca/etd/1812).
- Paney & Tharp (2019): 10 weeks of melody-singing training with real-time visual feedback + KR; "no evidence that this feedback created long-term dependency" and no group differences — as reported in [Blanco et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693/full).
- White (2025, *IJRCS*): computerised feedback for sight-singing "was no more effective than receiving no feedback at improving sight-singing achievement" — [White 2025 PDF](https://acda.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/IJRCSVol13White.pdf).
- Earlier studies found "a pattern of worsening results at the moment of receiving feedback", linked to the visual attention it demands — noted in [Blanco et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.684693/full) (consistent with Wilson's cognitive-load finding).
- Sing & See in teacher training: Leong & Cheng (2014, *J. Computer Assisted Learning*) pilot with pre-service music teachers in teacher-version, student-version and control groups — [Napier record](https://www.napier.ac.uk/research-and-innovation/research-search/outputs/effects-of-real-time-visual-feedback-on-pre-service-teachers-singing-effect-of-real-time); [DOI 10.1111/jcal.12046](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/jcal.12046); earlier user evaluations of WinSingad by two professional singing teachers (Howard et al. 2004; dated) — [NCVS abstract](https://ncvs.org/archive/pas/2004/abstracts/howard.htm); [PEVOC poster](https://pevoc.org/pevoc07/Daffern-poster.htm).
- Surveys: "A Survey on 30+ Years of Automatic Singing Assessment" (2026) — [arXiv 2601.12153](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.12153); "Automatic Detection and Analysis of Singing Mistakes for Music Pedagogy" (2026) — [arXiv 2602.06917](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06917).

**Interleaved and spaced practice**
- Carter & Grahn (2016, *Frontiers in Psychology*; 10 advanced clarinettists): interleaved practice "is generally found to impede performance during training compared to blocked practice, but to increase long-term learning"; participants reported better goal-setting and error detection yet "still preferred the blocked schedule"; authors call effects "modest" — [Carter & Grahn 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4989027/).
- Secondary-school replication (Veniel-Martí et al., *Update*, 2026): both schedules improved pre→post with no significant between-group difference; authors suggest teacher-directed interleaving may help retention — [citation record](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/comparing-interleaved-and-blocked-practices-in-secondary-school-music-performances/).
- Spacing for song memory: Katz, Ando & Wiseheart (2021, *Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications*) taught a two-verse song and varied inter-session gaps; spacing helped verbatim recall "once the spacing interval reaches a critical threshold" — [Katz et al. 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41235-021-00345-7); [PMC copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8665960); related York thesis "Optimizing Song Retention through Spacing" — [YorkSpace](https://yorkspace.library.yorku.ca/items/6e121c12-7bb8-4c3f-98f8-a70bd059afec).
- For motor skill specifically, one review states that "no data appear to exist that allow us to determine if a spacing effect exists for music skill retention" — [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5553926).

**Gamification**
- Troubadour (CSEDU 2024): inverse melodic/rhythmic dictation games with badges and leaderboards; engagement described, no outcome measure in the abstract — [SciTePress](https://scitepress.org/PublishedPapers/2024/127472).
- The Rhythmic Dictator (ISMIR 2020): gamified rhythm-dictation app A/B-tested with conservatory theory students; authors claim a "significant impact" on exam scores but the public abstract gives no n, groups or effect size — [ISMIR 2020 poster](https://program.ismir2020.net/poster_4-04.html).
- A 2024 within-subject bachelor thesis (Vienna) tested whether gamified elements in a practice app raise motivation (results not in excerpt) — [Flatz 2024](https://entertain.univie.ac.at/~hlavacs/theses/2024_Bachelor_Thesis_Flatz_Hanna.pdf); a 2024 *Technology, Pedagogy and Education* article is a content analysis of music games' affordances, not a trial — [TPE 2024](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/1475939X.2024.2319171).
- No controlled efficacy study of Yousician or Simply Piano was found: the literature is a 2024 Lancaster PhD autoethnography of Simply Piano plus adult interviews — [Gottler 2024](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/226859/3/2024gottlerphd.pdf); a one-week four-learner Yousician case study — [Yun & Thiruvarul 2021](https://shdl.mmu.edu.my/8764/); a three-month Portuguese school report concluding such tools are "an excellent complement to teaching, they cannot yet be considered as a potential substitute for a teacher" — [RCAAP report](https://comum.rcaap.pt/bitstreams/a6d1fb6b-5364-4445-bced-fb3f924ebe45/download). A widely repeated "34 % faster progress" figure comes from an uncited vendor-style blog — [The Maestro Online](https://www.the-maestro-online.com/blog/best-piano-apps-for-beginners/).

**Slowed playback and looping**
- No controlled study found. The only empirical item is a 2025 University of Waterloo analysis of 28 YouTube ear-learning lessons finding that "very few creators or viewers used looping or speed control, even though these tools have existed for two decades" (adoption, not efficacy) — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/05/250527124849.htm).
- Practitioner thresholds: Yamaha advises not going below ~75 % "because the audio starts to sound distorted" — [Yamaha](https://hub.yamaha.com/?p=29609); a drum-school blog puts the YouTube quality knee at ~80 % — [Loudlands](https://loudlands1.squarespace.com/blog/amazing-slow-downer); SlowGold's manual recommends raising tempo "in steps as small as 1%" once a passage is clean — [J.W. Pepper SlowGold](https://www.jwpepper.com/slowgold-cd-rom-10290638/p).

### Inferences
- Design to the one strong result: show the target *and* the learner's pitch *and* an explicit correct/incorrect verdict (KR). Contour-only "mirror" displays (Riyaz Smart Tanpura, Daily Riyaz) are pleasant but, per Welch and Blanco, do not by themselves produce learning.
- Build the fade-out in: because gains can decay when feedback is removed (the concern Blanco et al. discuss and the 2021 ETG pattern shows), schedule "blind" repetitions — play with the display hidden, then reveal — and track accuracy *without* feedback as the progress metric. This also addresses the oral-tradition worry about losing listening skill.
- Keep the display cheap to decode: Wilson's cognitive-load finding argues for one line against one bar, not a full spectrogram, during performance; richer views belong in review.
- A 50-cent "correct" threshold (Blanco) and a ~20-cent expert-level error are usable anchors for a pentatonic oud: green inside ±20–25 cents, amber to ±50, red beyond.
- Interleave *across songs within a sitting* only for returning learners; new learners should get blocked repetition (they prefer it and it does not hurt short-term). Spaced reminders are justified for repertoire memory (Katz 2021) but cannot be claimed for motor skill.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of slowed playback or looping on by-ear learning outcomes was found.
- Welch et al. 1989's actual accuracy numbers (results section) were not accessible (paywall; IOA PDF 404).
- Paney & Tharp 2019 and the Wilson et al. 2008 JIMS paper were only seen as cited by others.
- No meta-analysis of gamification in music education for 2024–2026 surfaced before the search budget ended.

## Key question 4 — Call-and-response ("listen and repeat") with automatic comparison: pitch-contour alignment (DTW), onset timing, scoring thresholds, and encouraging feedback design

### Takeaway
The standard pipeline is: extract F0 of the reference and the take, align with dynamic time warping, score intonation as deviation from the aligned reference (or from a semitone grid) and rhythm from aligned onset offsets, then combine; the literature's main cautions are that DTW on the pitch contour couples rhythm scoring to pitch errors, that pitch trackers mistake vibrato for instability, and that karaoke-style systems use ±1 semitone (±50 cents) windows that are too coarse for intonation teaching. Separate key-matching and rhythm-matching scores, and reference-free scoring from the known scale, are both patented/published alternatives.

### Cited Findings
- DTW-based measures are described as "specially simple and effective for intonation and rhythm assessment" in a 2013 Málaga study (as summarised; see the field surveys) — [Automatic singing assessment topic summary](https://api.emergentmind.com/topics/automatic-singing-assessment); [arXiv survey 2601.12153](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.12153).
- Known weakness: "DTW alignment on pitch contours ties rhythm scoring to pitch accuracy, so a singer with good timing but off-key pitch can be penalized on rhythm" (NUS, 2017) — [Perceptual Evaluation of Singing Quality](https://smcnus.comp.nus.edu.sg/archive/pdf/2017-2018/2017_Perceptual_Evaluation_of_Singing_Quality.pdf).
- Patent US 8,575,465: build pitch-contour representations, time-synchronise with the reference, produce **separate key-matching and rhythm-matching scores**, then combine — [Google Patents US8575465B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8575465).
- Patent US 8,634,759 ("timing offset tolerant karaoke game"): a note counts as correct if the sung pitch is "within a tolerance amount of pitch of the corresponding target note", and the tolerance can vary per word — [USPTO 8634759](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/8634759); US 11,341,946 scores each pitch against the reference across time units *and* time-offset units to absorb timing slop — [USPTO 11341946](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11341946).
- Nakano et al. (2006, Interspeech; dated) judge pitch-interval accuracy by "the fitting of the F0 trajectory to a semitone (100 cent) width grid", i.e. reference-free — [Nakano 2006](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2006/nakano06_interspeech.pdf).
- Reference-free scoring in 2025 uses the musical key instead of a reference vocal and warns that "pitch trackers can mistake vibrato for instability" — [Hsieh et al., Interspeech 2025](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/hsieh25c_interspeech.pdf); CNNs over a contour-vs-score distance matrix predict teacher ratings — [arXiv 2008.00203](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.00203.pdf); 2026 "music-aware" judge-style evaluation — [Listening Like a Judge](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.26451.md).
- Karaoke practice: an open-source developer compares the singer's pitch to the note "modulo octave, with a tolerance of 1 semitone", having studied UltraStar Deluxe — [Frank Karaoke write-up](https://akitaonrails.com/en/2026/04/05/turning-youtube-into-a-karaoke-app-frank-karaoke/); a Performous review: "singing within the right semitone consistently gives full points, so you may actually be off by a quartertone and still get the points" — [Performous review](https://www.junauza.com/2009/10/performous-another-karaoke-software-for.html); a scoring blog notes naive sequence comparison "is too sensitive to timing offsets" and recommends DTW plus onset detection — [Lilting](https://lilting.ch/en/articles/karaoke-scoring-revenge).
- Reference-vs-take display in the field: Riyaz draws the original singer's pitch and the learner's together in real time and scores the comparison (no threshold stated) — [Riyaz blog](https://riyazapp.com/blog/a-mirror-youve-never-seen-before/); Uyar & Bozkurt's usul trainer records, detects onsets and compares onset locations to the cycle template — [FMA 2015](https://zenodo.org/records/1211661/files/FMA2015_BurakUyar_FullPaper.pdf).
- Feedback tone: Melodics frames score as a personal per-lesson benchmark and treats Platinum as "a checkpoint" — [Melodics scoring](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/15092017-lesson-scoring); GarageBand lets the learner hide errors ("My Results … hides errors") — [GarageBand guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/garageband/gbndb71d9eec/mac); Daily Riyaz markets "no scores, no judgments" — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.rahuls.riyaz).

### Inferences
- For an oud call-and-response: (1) align with DTW on a *note-level* or onset-level representation, not raw F0, so that rhythm scoring is decoupled from intonation (the NUS caveat); (2) score intonation per note as median cents deviation from the fitted pentatonic degree (reference-free, like Nakano/Hsieh), with the teacher take used only to define the phrase and its rhythm; (3) score rhythm as onset offset after alignment, in ms and as a fraction of the beat.
- Thresholds to start from (all derived, not vendor-published): intonation green ≤ 25 cents, amber ≤ 50 cents (Blanco's "correct" boundary), red beyond; timing "great" ≤ 50 ms, "ok" ≤ 120 ms at beginner level (osu!taiko OD 0), tightened by level; treat octave errors as correct for singers (karaoke practice) but not for the oud.
- Encouraging design that survives the evidence: show the verdict (KR is what produces learning), but make the default score a per-phrase personal best, offer a hide-errors review toggle, and never penalise the rhythm score for an intonation miss.

### Gaps
- No published SingStar/UltraStar onset tolerance values or full scoring formulas were found; the 1-semitone pitch window is from secondary write-ups.
- The 2013 Málaga paper's own figures (which DTW variant, what correlation with judges) were not read; the NUS 2017 paper's numbers were not extracted.
- No study measuring how feedback *wording* (encouraging vs neutral) changes practice persistence in music apps surfaced.

## Key question 5 — Rhythm teaching: metronome designs, rhythm pads/games, compound and additive metres (dhaanto, buraanbur), and measuring tapping accuracy in the browser

### Takeaway
Good metronome trainers share four features — per-beat subdivisions and accents, a tempo ramp (start→end BPM by a step every N bars), muted bars ("coach" mode) to test internalised pulse, and polyrhythm overlays — and the pedagogy literature says compound metre is best introduced as 3/8 → 6/8 with a distinct syllable set, not by counting six. Human baselines matter for scoring: trained tappers anticipate the beat by ~10–30 ms and untrained by ~20–80 ms, synchronisation breaks down below ~150–200 ms inter-onset intervals, and browser round-trip audio latency is 40–100 ms and browser-dependent, so a browser rhythm game must calibrate latency per device before judging ±50 ms windows.

### Cited Findings

**Metronome / tempo-trainer designs (vendor listings)**
- Poly Metronome (iOS): tap tempo and dynamic accents free; subdivisions per beat and full polyrhythm paid; "Automator Mode lets you gradually increase tempo during practice"; "Coach Trainer mutes selected bars to test your rhythm" — [Poly Metronome](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1449133515).
- Takt (Android): triplets, sixteenths, swing subdivisions; per-beat accents; Tempo Trainer moves from start to end BPM by a step every chosen number of bars; "unstable metronome" mode "gently drifts around your target tempo. Your goal is to hold the true pulse instead of chasing the click" — [Takt](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.takt.app).
- Pro Metronome: Rhythm Trainer mutes one bar so the player fills the gap; Practice Mode schedules automatic tempo changes — [Pro Metronome](https://apps.apple.com/app/id477960671). Tempo Advance: up to 20 beats per measure with up to 20 subdivisions per beat, two simultaneous metres, Automator raising tempo by a set amount every set number of bars — [Tempo Advance](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/id368169363).
- Melodics' Auto BPM: +10 BPM per clean pass to natural tempo — [Melodics practice mode](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/6777027-practice-mode); Anytune's Step-it-Up loop trainer changes speed each repetition — [Anytune](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id415365180).

**Teaching compound metre**
- Telesco (JMTP): students "often count and notate compound meters as if they were simple meters"; introduce 3/8 before 6/8 — [Telesco, JMTP](https://journals.ou.edu/jmtp/ojs/ejournal/article/download/904/1795/1815).
- Takadimi (Hoffman, Pelto & White, 1996; dated): one syllable set for simple divisions, a separate set for compound; in compound metre "ta" marks the beat and "ki" and "da" its divisions — [Takadimi article, JMTP](https://journals.ou.edu/jmtp/article/download/748/1589/1579); lineage from Kodály — [Palkki 2010](https://journals.ou.edu/jmtp/article/download/649/1488/1479). The only comparison study found (2006 master's thesis) showed "no apparent difference in achievement" between Takadimi and another counting method — [Louisville thesis](https://ir.library.louisville.edu/etd/473).
- Non-Western precedent for syllable-based cycles: dum/tak skeleton with optional ornamentation ([Maqam World iqa'](https://www.maqamworld.com/en/iqaa.php)); solkattu recitation with teacher audio ([Carnatic Student](https://carnaticstudent.org/practice-four-talas)); theka at fixed laya ([Laya Tarang](https://www.bluestacks.com/campaign/com.siddhisadhana.layatarang/en)).

**Human tapping baselines (for calibrating scores)**
- Negative mean asynchrony (tapping ahead of the beat) is "often 10–30 vs. 20–80 ms" for amateur musicians vs nonmusicians (citing Aschersleben 2002; Repp & Doggett 2007) — [Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 2015 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2015.00154/pdf).
- Repp (2010): no difference in SD of asynchronies between amateurs and nonmusicians at 500 ms IOIs, so the musician advantage is not uniform — [same review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2015.00154/pdf); at very slow IOIs (1000–3500 ms) "nonmusicians showed much larger anticipation errors and higher variability" — [Tapping to a very slow beat](https://labs.sonicfield.org/library/tapping-to-a-very-slow-beat-a-comparison-of-musicians-and-nonmusicians).
- Lower limit of synchronisation ≈ 150–200 ms IOI for auditory stimuli vs ≈ 400 ms for visual (Repp 2003) — as cited in [London et al. 2019](https://jyx.jyu.fi/bitstream/123456789/66341/1/london2019tappingdoesnthelpsynchronizeds.pdf).
- Game windows for reference: osu!taiko GREAT ±50→20 ms, OK ±120→50 ms, MISS ±135→70 ms across OD 0→10 — [osu!taiko](https://osu.ppy.sh/wiki/en/Gameplay/Judgement/osu!taiko).

**Browser timing and latency**
- `AudioContext.outputLatency` "provides an estimate of audio hardware's output latency" (Bluetooth, USB interfaces vary); support Chrome 102, Edge 102, Firefox 70, Safari 18.4; use it to estimate when an audio timestamp reaches the listener — [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/audio-output-latency); [MDN outputLatency](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/AudioContext/outputLatency); `AudioPlaybackStats` exposes average/min/max latency and `resetLatency()` — [MDN AudioPlaybackStats](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioPlaybackStats); the need to map `AudioContext.currentTime` onto `performance.now()` was raised as W3C bug 20698 in 2013 — [W3C public-audio](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-audio/2013JanMar/0142.html).
- Measured round-trip (speaker→mic) latency, WAC 2025 MLS tool, 100 runs each: MacBook Pro 2021 — Chrome 52.33 ms (SD 1.14), Firefox 38.89, **Safari 100.02**; Windows 10 — Edge 60.82 (SD 6.06, range 55–96), Chrome 62.84, Firefox 104.65; Ubuntu 22.04 — Chrome 64.50 (SD 7.94, 49–85), Chromium 64.15, Firefox 65.69; Safari needs extra input-gain handling; success criterion correlation ratio > 18 dB — [weblatencytest (GitHub)](https://github.com/gilpanal/weblatencytest).
- Earlier measurements (2021): ~19 ms Chrome / ~14 ms Firefox on a 2016 MacBook with echo cancellation and noise suppression off, ~67 / ~55 ms with defaults; the `latency` constraint in getUserMedia "had no visible effect" and reported input latency "was not trustworthy" — [Jeff Kaufman, Browser Audio Latency](https://www.jefftk.com/p/browser-audio-latency); Mozilla's own bug used a beep-loopback method — [Bugzilla 1375466](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1375466).
- Soundslice ships an "Adjusting latency" help topic for its browser player — [Soundslice player help](https://soundslice.com/help/en/player).

### Inferences
- A browser Somali-rhythm trainer should: (a) represent each cycle as a stroke skeleton (low/high = dum/tak or clap types) with named cycles per genre rather than time signatures; (b) display the same cycle at two grouping levels (e.g. 12 pulses as 4×3 or 6×2) and let the annotator pick the felt grouping per song, which sidesteps the unresolved 4/4-vs-6/8 dhaanto question; (c) ship the four trainer features (subdivision/accent, ramp, muted-bar coach, drift mode) — they are table stakes in 2025 metronome apps.
- Scoring must subtract a per-device calibration: with Safari at ~100 ms round trip and Chrome at ~50 ms, an uncalibrated ±50 ms "great" window would mis-grade every Safari user. Run a loopback or tap-to-click calibration (as the WAC 2025 tool and Soundslice do), store the offset, and widen windows to the osu! OD-0 values (±50/±120 ms) for beginners.
- Expect learners to tap 20–80 ms early on average; show early/late bias as a separate, non-penalised indicator (Yousician's "early/late" labels) and penalise only variance at first.
- Do not run beat-level games faster than ~150–200 ms per judged event (≈ 300–400 BPM pulses); for fast dhaanto, judge at the beat, not the subdivision.

### Gaps
- No source on browser *input-event* timestamp precision (pointer/keyboard `Event.timeStamp` coarsening) was retrieved before the search budget ran out; from general knowledge Chrome coarsens `performance.now()` to ~100 µs and Firefox to 1 ms under resist-fingerprinting, but this is unverified here.
- Clone Hero / Guitar Hero / Rock Band ms windows: not published in reachable sources.
- No pedagogy source specific to Somali dhaanto or buraanbur rhythm teaching exists in the results; the one dance tutorial (4/4, claps on 1 and 3) is non-scholarly.
- Repp's 2005 review (the canonical source for tapping SD as a % of IOI) was paywalled; numbers above come from secondary citations.

## Key question 6 — Content structure: curriculum (lessons → exercises → songs), progress tracking, practice reminders, and rights-safe repertoire

### Takeaway
The apps structure content as path → unit → lesson → exercise → song, gate songs behind skills, score every performance into a per-song history, and hook retention to streaks and daily quests; repertoire is either licensed (and therefore removable), commissioned, or public-domain/educational-publisher material split into a cheaper tier. For a Somali studio the rights-safe path is the flowkey "Classic tier" pattern inverted: project-owned recordings, model output, and consented community recordings as the only served repertoire, which is exactly the constraint the archive already enforces.

### Cited Findings
- Duolingo Music: a learning path of units ("Unit 1: Learn the notes C, D, and E"), content "starting with individual notes and moving toward full-length songs"; XP, Daily Quests, achievements and streak apply to music lessons — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/p/fc0b9772-e1f6-4223-9a9f-ebf76b94b047/).
- Melodics: per-lesson scores are uncapped and lesson-specific, so the vendor instructs learners to "use your score as a personal benchmark for each lesson rather than comparing across the library"; stars 1–3 + Platinum as checkpoints — [Melodics scoring](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/15092017-lesson-scoring); progress explained in a separate article — [Melodics progress](https://support.melodics.com/en/articles/15091765-understanding-your-progress-in-melodics).
- GarageBand: per-lesson History graph with one point per complete performance, High Scores table with error areas, Reset Lesson — [GarageBand guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/garageband/gbnd50355d19/mac).
- Yousician: licensed pop songs since 2018; availability "subject to change" — [Yousician support](https://support.yousician.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000758937-Why-can-t-I-find-a-popular-song-in-Yousician); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yousician); repertoire includes "pieces specially commissioned by Yousician" — [Pianist Magazine, 10 apps](https://www.pianistmagazine.com/blogs/10-top-apps-to-help-improve-your-piano-playing/).
- flowkey: Classic tier = 1,000+ classical/public-domain pieces + theory/chords/rhythm/technique/reading courses; Premium = full licensed library — [flowkey Classic listing](https://deals.androidauthority.com/sales/flowkey-classic-plan-3-year-subscription).
- Simply Piano: pop arrangements carry major-publisher copyright notices (e.g. Sony Music Publishing, Universal) on sheet-music listings — [Musicnotes listing](https://api.musicnotes.com/marketplace/sheetmusic/MK0061582); 2026 OUP method-book deal "expanding Simply Piano's sheet music library across genres" — [OUP](https://corp.oup.com/?p=6927).
- Soundslice sells licensing/embedding for teachers' own lesson content rather than a song catalogue — [Soundslice licensing](https://soundslice.com/licensing); [Powered by Soundslice](https://www.soundslice.com/powered-by/).
- Ear-training products structure content as daily workouts and games (ToneGym) — [ToneGym about](https://tonegym.co/site/about); Functional Ear Trainer sequences by key context then degree — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1088761926).
- Oud for Guitarists sequences maqam study as hear → sing → know, selling audio + notation packs per maqam — [Oud for Guitarists](https://www.oudforguitarists.com/ear-training-program/); maqam practitioners sequence quarter-tone-free maqamat first — [Mike's Oud Forums](https://www.mikeouds.com/messageboard/viewthread.php?tid=12356).
- Retention evidence for reminders is limited to song memory: spacing helps verbatim recall above a threshold gap (Katz et al. 2021) — [Katz et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41235-021-00345-7); interleaving helps retention but learners prefer blocked practice — [Carter & Grahn 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4989027/).

### Inferences
- Curriculum skeleton for the oud: Path "Kaban basics" → units by scale degree set (1-2-3 → add 5 → add 6, mirroring Duolingo's three-note start and Chrome Music Lab's pentatonic grid) → lessons (listen, sing back, play back, play with cycle) → exercises (scored call-and-response phrases) → songs (showcase/consented clips with loop, slow-down, wait mode). Rhythm path in parallel: cycle recognition → clap-along with muted bars → play oud strokes to the cycle.
- Progress: store per-phrase personal bests (Melodics), a per-song history graph (GarageBand), and a *feedback-off* accuracy series as the headline metric (from the Blanco/Welch logic). Streaks and daily quests are the only retention mechanics with any adoption evidence here (Duolingo), and they are cheap.
- Rights: the archive's existing gate (only project-owned synthesis, model output and the rights-cleared clean-oud clips are served; Harvard/oud source audio never leaves the machine) already matches the industry's safest tier. A Learn mode can therefore use: the Listen-page showcase clips, generated reference phrases (the Generate tab), the browser-synthesised oud track, and musician-recorded phrases under a written consent record, with every item carrying a rights class like the model cards do.

### Gaps
- No vendor publishes retention/engagement numbers tied to reminders; the "practice reminder" effect in music apps is undocumented in sources found.
- No source describes Simply Piano's or Yousician's curriculum-gating rules (what unlocks a song) in detail.
- Licensing terms for pop catalogues in these apps are not public; only the fact of licensing and removability is documented.
