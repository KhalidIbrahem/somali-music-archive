# Recording guide: the oud takes for the separation and restoration demos

These recordings are Khalid's own playing, made for this project, so they can be
published. They are the only real audio on the portfolio. Three takes, about
five minutes of playing in total.

## Setup

- A quiet room with soft furniture. Fridge, fan and heating off. Phone on
  airplane mode.
- The best microphone to hand. A phone is fine: Voice Memos set to **Lossless**
  (Settings → Voice Memos → Audio Quality), phone on a stand 30 to 40 cm from
  the sound hole, not in the hand.
- Play at a normal level. The loudest notes must not distort: record ten
  seconds, listen back, move the phone further away if it crackles.
- Tune once and do not retune between takes.

## The takes

| take | what | length | why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Five seconds of silence, then a slow qaraami melody on solo oud, single notes | 60–90 s | restoration demo and the score beside a real recording; the silence measures the room noise |
| 2 | A faster piece or the same melody with ornaments | 60–90 s | a harder case for the transcriber |
| 3 | Voice: wear headphones, play take 1 in them, and sing or hum the melody over it. Record **only the voice** | same length as take 1 | take 1 + take 3 mixed is a voice-and-oud recording whose true parts are known, so the separation can be scored exactly |

Take 3 matters most for the separation demo. If singing is not possible, a
second oud line or a hand drum recorded the same way also works.

## Hand-over

- Keep the original files, untouched: no trimming, no noise reduction, no
  normalising. WAV, M4A lossless or FLAC; not MP3.
- Put them in `~/Projects/somali-music-archive/data/raw_incoming/khalid_oud_2026-11/`
  named `take1_oud.*`, `take2_oud.*`, `take3_voice.*`.
- Add one line saying what the melody is and whether it is a traditional tune
  or a known composer's song. A song with a living composer or their heirs may
  need its own permission before the performance is published; a traditional
  melody or an improvisation does not.
