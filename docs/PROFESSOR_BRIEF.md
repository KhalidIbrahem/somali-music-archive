## What this is

The first dedicated, labeled dataset of Somali music for computational research — built from the **Maryan "Aryette" Omar Ali Collection** at Harvard's Archive of World Music (AWM Spec Coll 103): 504 cassettes of Somali song recorded 1955–1991, cataloged at track level, digitized by Harvard. Somali music currently has **zero** representation in the music-information-retrieval literature; even Ethiopian music has had a labeled dataset since 2023. I have built the corpus infrastructure, processed a 105-track / 22.6-hour development subset end-to-end, and drafted the ISMIR dataset paper.

## What is already done (all reproducible from code, 129 unit tests)

- **Corpus + schema.** 605 digitized tracks inventoried and joined to Harvard's CC BY 4.0 catalog: titles, performers, cassette/side provenance, and — critically — **exact recording dates recovered for a subset** (e.g. *"Milgo, 1966-08-25"*), which is what makes historical analysis possible.
- **A measurement method for archival intonation.** Cassette audio confounds tuning measurement: playback-speed error and unknown keys masquerade as "microtonality." My pipeline fits a pentatonic reference grid *per track*, absorbing key, tape speed, and A440 assumptions in one parameter. The effect is decisive: deviations that smear to **63 cents** against a fixed grid concentrate to a **26-cent median dispersion with 86% of frames within a quarter tone** after alignment (105 tracks, 706,483 analyzed frames) — evidence the method measures the music, not the tape deck. (That 26 cents sits within two cents of the ~24-cent universal vocal-imprecision constant reported cross-culturally in *Scientific Reports* — Somali song lands where the world's traditions land.)
- **A real historical question, with a preliminary answer.** Did Somali intonation regularize toward equal temperament as organs and electric guitars entered the ensembles? On date-stamped tracks (1964–1976): **stability, not drift** (p = 0.47, n = 19 — preliminary, honestly powered). The full 605-track corpus extends the test across 1955–1991.
- **Honest engineering.** Resumable pipeline (quality audit → enhancement → separation → Whisper Somali transcription with sung-hallucination gating → CREPE pitch → MERT embeddings); cassette-level evaluation splits to prevent leakage; a validated JSON Schema; every number in the paper generated by committed analysis code.

## Why it matters

1. **Preservation with a deadline.** The tradition survives on decaying tape and in the memories of elderly performers; Somali-led efforts (Redsea Cultural Foundation, Hargeysa) are documenting it in books — this project gives the same effort computational scale.
2. **Science.** A dated, single-tradition corpus spanning the arrival of equal temperament is a natural experiment in how tonal systems respond to instrument change — answerable for the first time for the Horn of Africa.
3. **MIR robustness.** Real archival audio (median SNR 9.5 dB) is a stress test the field's benchmarks avoid.
4. **Language technology.** Sung Somali is a hard, underserved ASR frontier; the corpus doubles as its benchmark.
